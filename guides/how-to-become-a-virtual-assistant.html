How to become a virtual assistant

A virtual assistant helps businesses with work that can be done from anywhere: email, calendars, customer messages, social media, data and admin. You need reliable internet, good written English and the habit of finishing what you start. Everything else can be learned.

What virtual assistants actually do

  • Admin: inbox management, calendar booking, travel, documents and data entry.
  • Customer support: replying to emails, chats and comments, and tracking orders.
  • Social media: scheduling posts, simple graphics, replying to comments.
  • Sales support: finding leads, updating a CRM, sending follow-ups.
  • Specialist work: bookkeeping, design, video editing, websites, research.

The skills clients look for first

  1. Clear written communication. Short, polite, correct messages build trust faster than anything.
  2. Reliability. Doing what you said, by when you said, and telling the client early if something slips.
  3. Tool confidence. Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, a task tool such as Trello or Asana, and a video call app.
  4. Good judgment. Knowing what you can decide alone and what to check with the client.

Your path to a first client

  1. Pick a starting service. Choose one or two things you already do well, for example inbox management and scheduling.
  2. Set up your workspace. A quiet place, a headset, a backup internet option, a password manager and two-step sign-in on every account.
  3. Build a small portfolio. Make two or three practice samples: a cleaned-up spreadsheet, a sample email reply set, a week of planned social posts.
  4. Create your profile. A clear photo, a one-line headline, a short about section, your skills, tools and samples. You can create a free Delegance profile and share its link with every application.
  5. Apply with focus. Read each job post carefully and reply to what that client needs, not with a copy-paste message.
  6. Over-deliver on the first task. Your first client becomes your first reference.

Protect yourself and your clients

Never share passwords in chat. Ask clients to add you to tools with your own login, or to share access through a password manager. Keep client files in the client's own storage, not on your personal drive. Good security habits are part of being professional.

Checklist before you apply:
☐ Headline that says what you do
☐ Two or three work samples
☐ Tools you can use, listed honestly
☐ Hours you can work, with time zone
☐ Two-step sign-in on your email
