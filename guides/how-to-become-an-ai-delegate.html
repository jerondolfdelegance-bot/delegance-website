How to become an AI Delegate

AI can now draft, sort, research and follow up on its own. What businesses still need is a person who knows how to point it at the right work, catch its mistakes and take responsibility for the result. That person is an AI Delegate.

What an AI Delegate does

  • Sets the AI up: gives it the business's voice, rules and examples, and connects it to the tools the client already uses.
  • Runs the daily work: inbox, support replies, posts, research, reports and follow-ups, with AI doing the first draft.
  • Checks every step: nothing reaches a customer until the Delegate has read it, fixed it and approved it.
  • Reports back: a short, plain update on what was done and what needs the owner.

The skills that matter

  1. Clear briefing. Writing instructions an AI (or a person) can follow is the core skill. Our five-part brief works for both.
  2. Judgement. Spotting when an answer is wrong, off-brand or risky, and knowing when to ask the owner.
  3. Tool fluency. Comfort with the AI assistants, automation and business apps your clients use, learned from their official help centres.
  4. Safe access. Working through proper team invites instead of shared passwords. See how to keep accounts safe.

A simple 30-day plan

  1. Week 1: pick one task you already do well, like inbox sorting or post captions, and do it with an AI assistant. Compare the time and quality.
  2. Week 2: write a reusable brief for that task, with the client's tone and three good examples.
  3. Week 3: add a checking step: a short list of what you verify before anything goes out.
  4. Week 4: turn it into a portfolio project: the task, the tools, your checks and the time saved.

Be honest about AI

Tell clients which parts AI helps with and which parts you check yourself. Never paste private client data into a tool the client has not approved. Trust is the reason businesses hire a person to run AI instead of running it alone.

Show it on your profile

Add the AI tools you use and the project that proves it to your free Delegance profile. Clients browsing candidates can then see exactly what you can run for them.
