What to hand to a virtual assistant first

The best first tasks for a virtual assistant are the ones you do often, that follow the same steps each time, and that you secretly dread. Start there and your first week already gives you hours back.

Start with a one-week time list

For one week, jot down every small task that takes you away from the work only you can do. Don't judge them yet. At the end of the week, mark the ones that:

  • happen every day or every week
  • follow roughly the same steps each time
  • don't need your personal judgement or signature

Those marked tasks are your hand-over list.

Six good first tasks

  1. Inbox triage. Sorting, labelling and archiving email, and flagging the few messages that really need you.
  2. Calendar and bookings. Scheduling meetings, sending reminders and handling reschedules.
  3. Customer questions. Answering common questions from chat, email and comments with replies you've approved.
  4. Social media posting. Scheduling posts you've already written, or drafting them for you to check.
  5. Data and lists. Updating spreadsheets, contact lists and simple reports.
  6. Research. Comparing suppliers, finding leads or summarising options so you can decide faster.

What to keep for yourself, for now

Keep anything that needs a licence, a legal signature or access to money until trust is built. That includes payments, contracts and anything medical, legal or financial. A good VA will tell you when a task belongs with you.

Hand it over in three steps

  1. Show it once. Record your screen while you do the task, or do it together on a call.
  2. Write the rules. A short checklist: what good looks like, what to avoid, and when to ask you.
  3. Review the first few. Check the first week's work closely, then step back once it's right.

Most people are surprised how quickly the hand-over pays off. The first week is the slowest; after that, the hours come back every week.
